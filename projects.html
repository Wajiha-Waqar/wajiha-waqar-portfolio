Project 01

MVP testedExtensions planned

Research Paper Assistant Workflow

  • Research
  • AI / ML
  • Systems
Problem
Academic research means switching between several AI tools and research sites for literature review, summarization, drafting and citation organization, while every claim must stay grounded in real papers.
Approach
A structured workflow, classified as prompt-chaining rather than an autonomous agent. Each tool has one responsibility: NotebookLM grounds answers in uploaded PDFs, Claude reasons and drafts, ChatGPT improves formatting. Inputs, outputs, responsibilities and tool-access boundaries are defined.
Methods
Academic-integrity rules in a draft system prompt, five evaluation cases, risks and guardrails, and a four-iteration build log. The MVP output was evaluated for citation accuracy, unsupported claims, summarization quality and formatting.
Outcome
An MVP tested on five research papers. Human verification remains necessary. Zotero integration, GitHub storage and automatic citation management are planned and not implemented.

Project 02

Completed

Leakage-Aware Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

  • Research
  • AI / ML
  • Data
Problem
Content teams have more pages worth reviewing than they can inspect. Can March-only, non-label-derived search and engagement signals rank content that later meets a predefined refresh-review condition in April?
Approach
A predictive ranking and triage system to support human review, not automated publishing. Month-to-month development panels (Jan→Feb, Feb→Mar) and a held-out future test window (Mar→Apr).
Methods
26 engineered features from search position, GA4 engagement and traffic-channel signals; a leakage audit; Logistic Regression and Random Forest compared with client-grouped validation and selected on Average Precision; a tiered review queue with reason codes.
Outcome
Random Forest outperformed a fixed Week-4 baseline on the held-out month (Precision@20 0.65 against 0.40; Average Precision 0.4838 against 0.3807). This shows improved ranking performance, not a causal effect of refreshing content.

Project 03

Research in progressDesign and defense proposed

Evaluating Cross-Session Prompt Injection in MCP-Based AI Agents: Persistence, Security Defenses, and Tool-Use Outcomes

  • Research
  • AI security
  • Systems
Problem
LLM agents that act through tools can be manipulated by attacker-controlled content (prompt injection). This project studies a cross-session variant: attacker-controlled content held in persistent application state that survives a session boundary.
Question
Does attacker-controlled persistent state that survives a session boundary change the likelihood of unintended MCP-mediated actions?
Defense
Proposed: deterministic provenance and trust policies that identify untrusted persistent content before sensitive tool execution.
Status
Research in progress. The experimental design and defense concept are proposed. No results, publications or performance claims are reported.
